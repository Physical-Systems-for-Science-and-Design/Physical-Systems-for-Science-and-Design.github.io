About

Physical simulation underpins work from protein dynamics and climate modeling to robotic control and metamaterial optimization. However, high-fidelity physical methods still struggle to scale: across spatial and temporal resolutions, across the sim-to-real divide, and across the diversity of real-world physical regimes. Machine learning provides expressive surrogates and neural operators, but often at the cost of physical consistency, numerical stability, and generalizability.

This workshop brings together researchers from machine learning, physics-based simulation, computer graphics, and computational design to examine the principles and methods that enable physical modeling to scale reliably.

Call for Papers

We invite submissions across two non-archival tracks managed through OpenReview:

  • Short Papers (4 pages): Novel architectures, empirical evaluations, theoretical insights, and reusable physical simulation platforms.
  • Tiny Papers (2 pages): Early-stage ideas, negative results, failure analyses, and fresh perspectives. Per ICLR guidelines, AI-generated papers are prohibited in the Tiny Papers track.

Accepted contributions will be presented as spotlights or posters. Submissions are non-archival and will not preclude publication elsewhere.

Topics of Interest

  • Resolution Scaling: Neural operators, multi-scale solvers, discretization invariance, rollout stability, conservation laws.
  • Domain Scaling: Geometric deep learning, Lie group equivariance, scientific foundation models, out-of-distribution generalization.
  • Reality Scaling: Differentiable physics, sim-to-real transfer in robotics and fabrication, system identification, contact modeling.
